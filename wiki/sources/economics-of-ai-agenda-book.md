## TL;DR

The definitive academic agenda for economic research on AI. This volume collects contributions from 30+ leading economists (including Acemoglu, Brynjolfsson, Aghion, Stiglitz, Milgrom, Varian, Kahneman, Goolsbee) organized around four themes: AI as a general-purpose technology; growth, jobs, and inequality; regulation and industrial organization; and machine learning's impact on economic research itself.

## Why It's Here

Where *Prediction Machines* (2018) is the business-intuition book and *The Microeconomics of AI* (2025) is the single-author rigorous treatment, this 2019 volume is the **multi-author research agenda** that scoped the field. It's like being back in 1995 when the internet was emerging and economists gathered to debate what would happen — except this time it's AI.

## Key Themes and Chapters

### I. AI as a GPT (General Purpose Technology)
1. **AI and the Modern Productivity Paradox** — Brynjolfsson, Rock, Syverson
2. **The Technological Elements of AI** — Matt Taddy
3. **Prediction, Judgment, and Complexity** — Agrawal, Gans, Goldfarb
4. **AI's Impact on Innovation** — Cockburn, Henderson, Stern
5. **Needles in Haystacks: AI and Recombinant Growth** — Agrawal, McHale, Oettl
6. **AI as the Next GPT: Political Economy** — Manuel Trajtenberg

### II. Growth, Jobs, and Inequality
7. **AI, Income, Employment, and Meaning** — Betsey Stevenson
8. **AI, Automation, and Work** — Daron Acemoglu & Pascual Restrepo
9. **AI and Economic Growth** — Philippe Aghion, Benjamin Jones, Charles Jones
10. **AI and Jobs: The Role of Demand** — James Bessen
11. **Public Policy in an AI Economy** — Austan Goolsbee
12. **Should We Be Reassured?** — Jason Furman
13. **R&D, Structural Transformation, and Income** — Jeffrey Sachs
14. **AI and Income Distribution** — Anton Korinek & Joseph Stiglitz
15. **Neglected Open Questions** — Tyler Cowen

### III. Machine Learning and Regulation
16. **AI, Economics, and Industrial Organization** — Hal Varian
17. **Privacy, Algorithms, and AI** — Catherine Tucker
18. **AI and Consumer Privacy** — Ginger Jin
19. **AI and International Trade** — Avi Goldfarb & Daniel Trefler
20. **Punishing Robots: Tort Liability** — Alberto Galasso & Hong Luo

### IV. Machine Learning and Economics
21. **ML's Impact on Economics** — Susan Athey
22. **AI, Labor, Productivity, and Firm-Level Data** — Manav Raj & Robert Seamans
23. **AI and Market Design** — Paul Milgrom & Steven Tadelis
24. **AI and Behavioral Economics** — Colin Camerer (with Daniel Kahneman comment)
