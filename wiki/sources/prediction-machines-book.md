## TL;DR

The original business book that reframed AI as fundamentally about prediction. Instead of treating AI as magic or general intelligence, the authors analyze it through a simple economic lens: AI makes prediction dramatically cheaper, which reshapes everything that depends on it — decision-making, judgment, policy, strategy.

## Key Claims

1. **AI is a prediction technology** — The core advance is making statistical prediction cheap, fast, and accessible. This is the foundation for understanding its economic impact.
2. **The value of prediction is in how it changes decisions** — Prediction is valuable precisely because it enables better decisions, not because it's interesting in isolation.
3. **Three forces shape AI value** — Data (improves prediction quality), judgment (needed to act on predictions), and action (what you do with improved decisions).
4. **AI reduces uncertainty** — By lowering the cost of prediction, AI reduces uncertainty, which changes the economics of inventory, risk, monitoring, and compliance across industries.
5. **Strategies emerge from understanding complements** — The real strategic value comes from identifying what complements cheap prediction: data, infrastructure, talent, and judgment.

## Relationship to Gans's 2025 MIT Book

*The Microeconomics of Artificial Intelligence* (2025) is the rigorous academic follow-up. Where *Prediction Machines* is aimed at business leaders and strategists, the 2025 book is a full microeconomic treatment with formal models, proofs, and chapters on pricing, market structure, and policy.
