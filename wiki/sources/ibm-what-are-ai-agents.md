## Summary

This IBM Technology video defines AI agents as a progression from monolithic models to compound AI systems. Maya Murad explains how agents integrate LLMs with databases and external tools to solve problems, contrasting programmatic control logic (e.g., RAG) with agentic approaches where the LLM itself reasons and plans. She breaks down the ReACT framework (Reason + Act) and illustrates the trade-offs between programmatic efficiency and agentic flexibility.

## Key Claims

- **Compound AI systems are here to stay** — combining models with tools, databases, and programmatic components yields more powerful solutions than models alone.
- **Agentic systems trade efficiency for flexibility** — for narrow well-defined problems, programmatic control is more efficient; for complex open-ended tasks, agentic approaches are better.
- **Three core capabilities of agents**:
  1. **Reasoning** — the LLM breaks down problems and plans steps.
  2. **Acting** — the LLM calls external tools (search, calculator, APIs).
  3. **Memory** — storing conversation history and internal reasoning logs.
- **ReACT = Reason + Act** — an agentic pattern where the LLM alternates between thinking (reasoning) and doing (tool calls), observing results, and iterating until the answer is found.
- **Human in the loop** — most agent systems today still include human oversight as accuracy improves.
